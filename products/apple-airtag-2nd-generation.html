Apple AirTag (2nd Generation), Single

What it is

This listing is a single second-generation Apple AirTag, a small disc you attach to keys, a bag, a wallet, or another item you want to find later. AirTags are designed to work with Apple's Find My network and, on compatible iPhones, Precision Finding. A single tag is a way to add one item at a time rather than buying a multi-pack. It is a tracker accessory, not headphones or a charger.

Who it is for

An AirTag suits people in the Apple ecosystem who want to keep track of keys, a backpack, or luggage. It is less useful if your household is mostly Android, because the finding features are built around Apple's network and apps. It is also not a GPS pet tracker with its own cellular plan. If you need to tag several items at once, a multi-pack may be more convenient than repeating a single-tag purchase.

Features to check before you buy

  • That this is a single second-generation AirTag, not a four-pack.
  • Apple's current compatibility notes, including which iPhones support Precision Finding.
  • Battery and replacement notes on the product page. AirTags use a user-replaceable battery in typical designs, but you should confirm the current details.
  • How you will attach it. A bare tag needs a key ring, case, or holder for many uses.
  • Privacy and safety features described by Apple, including alerts if an unknown tag is moving with you.

Pros

  • A single tag lets you start with one item instead of a full pack.
  • It is designed to work with the Find My network already used by many iPhone owners.
  • The disc is small enough for keys, bags, and similar everyday items.
  • Second-generation details, including finding features, should be checked on the current Apple or Amazon page so you know what your phone supports.

Tradeoffs

  • You may still need to buy a case or loop to attach the tag securely.
  • The finding experience is built for Apple devices, so it is a poor fit as a household standard if most people around you use other phones.
  • It will not replace a dedicated pet or vehicle tracker with live cellular location.
  • A single tag is less economical if you already know you want to mark several items at once.

How it compares

An AirTag is not an alternative to the audio products on this shelf. The USB-C EarPods, AirPods 5, and AirPods Pro 3 are for listening. Choose an AirTag if your problem is lost items, not sound. If you are deciding between a single tag and a pack, buy the single if you only have one item to mark, and consider a pack only when you already have several items waiting.

Buying tip

Check that your iPhone supports the finding features you care about, and budget for a holder if you plan to put the tag on a key ring rather than dropping a bare disc into a bag.
